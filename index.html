Custom software

When it makes sense

Your existing software doesn’t support how the operation actually runs — or you’re running the business out of spreadsheets. Most off-the-shelf tools fit a generic business; yours has its own workflow, its own rules, and its own data.

What gets built

Internal business applications, operational dashboards, customer portals, CRM and operations systems, custom web applications, and the databases and backend systems underneath them.

Automation

When it makes sense

A defined process eats hours every week and doesn’t need human judgment at every step — copying between systems, manual data entry, lead follow-up, recurring reports, updating three places instead of one. That is work a computer can do.

What gets built

Workflow automation, data processing, lead management, notifications and follow-ups, reporting, and system-to-system API integrations. I run systems like these in my own company every day — including the stack that replaced my paid SDR tooling.

AI

When it makes sense

The work involves reading, extracting, drafting, or sorting at a volume no person should be doing manually. It is not magic, and I won’t sell it as such — if a plain automation solves the problem, that’s what you’ll get.

What gets built

Document and data processing, classification and extraction, assistants that answer from your business data, natural-language interfaces to your systems, and AI integrated into the software you already run. I use LLM APIs and AI agent runtimes in daily production in my own business.

The tools follow the problem: JavaScript, Python, Next.js, Node.js, NestJS, PostgreSQL, Supabase, REST APIs, n8n, Apify, Docker, and LLM APIs. This list is here so you know the work is real — not so you have to choose.

Not every business problem needs another automation tool.

Architecture is chosen from the actual business problem, never forced into one tool.

If I don’t think software is the answer, I’ll say so on the first call.

Most service businesses don’t have an AI problem. They have a systems problem disguised as a tool problem. They buy a tool, it works for the happy path, then something edge-case happens and it breaks — because they didn’t need a tool. They needed a system.

So I start from the problem, not the tool. Sometimes the answer is custom software. Sometimes it’s a workflow connecting systems you already pay for. Sometimes it’s AI where judgement is needed and plain code where it isn’t. Sometimes the right move is deleting a step instead of automating it.

When it’s done, I want you to say “They rebuilt how our business runs.” — not “They built us an n8n workflow.”

How I work

Understand → Design → Build → Deploy → Maintain. You work with me directly the whole way — the person who maps the problem is the person who builds the system.

  1. 01

    Understand

    You show me how the process works today. I ask the awkward questions and map where time and money leak.

  2. 02

    Design

    You get the plan before any code: the data model, the workflow, what it replaces. Nothing starts until that’s agreed.

  3. 03

    Build

    Core logic is tested first, then integrated. You see it take shape at each stage — not a black box that appears at the end.

  4. 04

    Deploy

    It goes live against real data, connected to the tools you already run, with documentation your team can actually use.

  5. 05

    Maintain

    Systems drift as businesses change. I stay on for fixes, changes, and the next stage once this one is running.
